The Slavers’ Pact is a loose confederation of slave masters formed through shared interest rather than law. It enables trade, exchange, and coordination between its members and has no central authority or binding legislation.

It functions as a culture of practice rather than a formal state. Agreements are maintained through expectation and consequence, and disputes are handled through negotiation and leverage between parties.

Envoys are appointed to handle communication and external representation. They act as intermediaries between members and as a unified voice to outsiders.

The Slavers’ Pact can therefore not be considered a formal entity, but a sustained network of cooperation.
