# League of Dukes Guide #
*League of Dukes* is part of *Seats of Power*: a collection of additional dwarven government types.

## General features of League of Dukes ##
Main features:
* **Independent duchies, ruled by councils and united in a league.**
* **Elected aldermen for each discipline form the local executive branch.**
* **The archduke is an elevated landholder position, and acts as head of the league.**

The League of Dukes is a confederation of loosely connected cities, each governed by a council of dukes. Aldermen are elected officials responsible for specific areas of governance within each city. These cities are united in a league that fosters cooperation and mutual support while maintaining autonomy. The League is characterized by its intricate political structure, where power is distributed among various offices, creating a dynamic political landscape.

Along the coasts of oceans and great lakes, cities of the League thrive on agriculture and trade. The League's culture is rich and diverse, with a strong emphasis on diplomacy and political intrigue.

Upon reaching certain population thresholds, the League promotes a grand duke to archduke. The archduke serves as the highest authority, providing leadership and guidance while respecting the autonomy of member cities.

The inspiration of the League of Dukes is drawn from the setting of D&D's *Baldur's Gate*, where the city is a prominent member of the *League of Lords*, ruled by the *council of four* and the *parliament of peers*. These political structures are loosely reflected in the mod.

### Challenge level: 3/5 ###
'League of Dukes' is designed to be a moderate challenging experience. 

Harder:
* The election system places citizens in positions of power with limited player control.
* The complex ducal and alderman structure requires careful management of demands and resources.
* Increased *cunning* and decreased *law* values lead to more intrigue and criminality.

Easier:
* Trade caravans arrive more frequently and bring a wider variety of goods.
* Additional clerks can be appointed to support aldermen in their duties.

### My personal opinion ###
I think this entity is pretty cool, especially the elected aldermen. This gives a real sense of local politics and has you as a player managing the demands of your elected officials, which is a fun challenge. Also, I really like the Archduke (monarch) position, that is created after your site reaches a certain threshold. The ducal hierarchy and the council of dukes add an interesting layer of complexity to the government structure.

  
## Play Guide ##
### Aldermen and the Council of Dukes ###
* At certain population levels, citizens can be elected as aldermen.
* Aldermen oversee specific domains such as trade, security, production, and finance. Each alderman has their own set of responsibilities and demands that must be met to maintain their support.
* Due to game mechanics, a single unit may hold multiple alderman positions, concentrating power. 
* When an alderman becomes a duke, they temporarily lose their alderman roles but may be re-elected immediately.
* Most alderman roles favor strong social skills, often resulting in the same individuals being elected for multiple positions. This can lead to a concentration of power in the hands of socially adept individuals.
* As your settlement grows, the demands of the aldermen will increase. It is important to regularly check their demands and ensure they are met to maintain their support and prevent unrest.
* At higher population levels, a high alderman can be appointed, later becoming the first duke. Eventually, a council of up to five dukes forms: the presiding land-holder and up to four additional co-dukes.

### Archduke ###
* At landholder level 5, the grand duke becomes an archduke, the equivalent of a monarch. The archduke appoints league-level positions, but these are based in the capital and only become available and visible to you once your fortress becomes the capital.
* Promotion mechanics: During this promotion, the Grand Duke first passes through the Duke-Protector position and then immediately becomes Archduke. The Duke-Protector position is left vacant and is subsequently filled by another eligible citizen. As a result, the player sees their Grand Duke become Archduke while a new Duke-Protector appears to represent the city in the capital.

### Other notes ###
* Despite the name 'League of Dukes', civilizations use generated names (e.g., "The Great Group of Nobles").
* Clerks can be appointed from certain population levels for tasks such as trading (*clerk of stores*), management (*clerk of works*), and accounting (*clerk of coin*). 
* The promotion official can be used to force reevaluation of noble positions and trigger the first promotions. 
* For justice, the alderman warden and alderman inquisitor work together: The warden maintains order and oversees prisons, the inquisitor interrogates suspects.
* There are three types of executioners (axe, hammer, spear) that can be appointed by the archduke once your settlement has become the capital. You can choose which method of execution you prefer, and appoint the corresponding executioner type.
* The *high authority* is a placeholder for the leader, replaced by the first archduke, once the position is filled.
* Expect increased intrigue and criminal activity, especially relevant for adventurer players.
* Caravans arrive in summer and autumn.
* Production options for clothing and weapons are expanded, and they make use of all crop types.
* Custom naming for groups, roads, bridges, and more.
* No DFHack is required.
* Dwarf-only entity; works with vanilla dwarves.
* Compatibility with other mods is assumed, not guaranteed.

### Starting a new fortress ###
* Choose a civilization with a name containing something like "League". The "high authority" position should preferably be vacant (preferably), unless you intend to play an already established archducal city. When generating long-lived worlds, it is likely that this position will already be filled.
* On embark, you only have a *chartermaster* and the open position of *election official* available. 
* Appoint an election official after the first migrants arrive to trigger alderman elections
* These civilizations may be destroyed early during world generation. Ensure at least one survives if you plan to play them.

## Government structure ##
**The First Aldermen** are two elected positions representing the League's founding authority. They form a special founding office, separate from the ten ordinary alderman roles described below. They hold law-making power and receive diplomats on behalf of the League as a whole. As the League matures, they are succeeded by the **League Secretary** — an elected administrator who manages diplomatic correspondence, appoints the League Emissary and the League Envoy, and maintains inter-city communication.

**The Archduke** is the supreme authority of the League. The position is filled when a Grand Duke's city reaches the highest prosperity threshold (land-holder level 5), elevating that grand duke to the archducal seat. The Archduke holds law-making authority, receives diplomats, and sets military goals for the League. All league-level ministerial appointments flow from the Archduke:

- The **Ducal Marshal** commands League military forces and directs captains across the realm.
- The **Ducal Ambassador** conducts diplomacy: making introductions, brokering peace agreements, and negotiating topic accords with foreign powers.
- The **Ducal Justiciar** enforces law across the ducal cities, adjudicates disputes, and oversees tax collection, supported by a personal squad of constables.
- The **Ducal Treasurer** manages the Archducal finances: accounting, trade revenues, and coin design.

When the League's capital is established, the Archduke also appoints the settlement's **Executioner** — available in three forms (hammer, axe, or spear), the choice determining the method of capital punishment.

**The Ducal Hierarchy** tracks the growth of each city through five successive land-holder tiers:

- A **High Alderman** (LH1) marks official recognition as a League Town. The high alderman receives diplomats and holds the settlement together until ducal status is reached.
- A **Duke** (LH2) presides over a ducal city, exercising law-making, judicial, and diplomatic authority. The duke appoints the first site-level co-dukes and a League Emissary.
- A **High Duke** (LH3) chairs the Council of Dukes of their city. They may appoint an additional co-duke and a High Emissary.
- A **Grand Duke** (LH4) adds military authority to the council's mandate and may appoint a Grand Emissary.
- A **Duke-Protector** (LH5 onward) relocates to the capital to represent their city's interests at the League level. It is the Duke-Protector who appoints the Archduke once the threshold is met.

**The Council of Dukes** is the governing body of each ducal city. It consists of the presiding land-holder and up to four additional site-level co-dukes, for a maximum of five dukes in total. These co-dukes — each drawn from the ranks of sitting aldermen — share law-making, judicial, and diplomatic responsibilities. When a ducal seat falls vacant, succession passes through the aldermanic rolls.

**The Aldermen** are the elected officials of daily governance. Ten distinct alderman roles exist, each covering a specific domain of city management. All are elected and scale through four tiers of authority and demands as the settlement's population grows (roughly at populations 8, 62–80, 122–140, and 182–200).

- The **Alderman Chamberlain** is the first elected official (from population 8). They receive diplomats and manage the household of the settlement's senior leadership.
- The **Alderman Quartermaster** manages trade and procurement. They appoint the *Clerk of Stores*, who acts as broker.
- The **Alderman Warden** is responsible for law enforcement and local military strategy. They command a squad of constables and appoint site captains.
- The **Alderman Industrialist** oversees production, construction permits, and food supply. They appoint the *Clerk of Works*, who acts as manager.
- The **Alderman Treasurer** handles accounting and tax collection and determines local coin design. They appoint the *Clerk of Coin*, who acts as bookkeeper.
- The **Alderman Confessor** (from population 20) manages the spiritual and moral welfare of the settlement: meeting workers, overseeing religious rites, and building morale.
- The **Alderman Physician** (from population 20) is responsible for public health across the settlement.
- The **Alderman Emissary** (from population 20) maintains roads and bridges and handles inter-settlement correspondence. They appoint the *Clerk of Letters*, who acts as messenger.
- The **Alderman Inquisitor** (from population 50) investigates crimes and uncovers plots, working alongside the warden in law enforcement and espionage.
- The **Alderman Beastmaster** (from population 50) oversees animal populations, welfare, and the taming of exotic creatures.

**The Clerks** are appointed support staff attached to specific alderman offices. The **Clerk of Stores** (broker), **Clerk of Works** (manager), **Clerk of Coin** (bookkeeper), and **Clerk of Letters** (messenger) each require a functioning market and handle the practical implementation of their alderman's mandate.

**Captains** are appointed by the Alderman Warden at the site level, and by the Ducal Marshal at the League level. Each commands a squad of ten soldiers, with responsibilities including patrolling territory, escorting tax collectors, and engaging enemies.

**The Chartermaster** is the founding official of a new settlement. They hold trade and diplomatic authority from the moment of embark, command a small constable squad, and appoint the **Election Official** — a functional position used to trigger the first aldermanic elections. Once elections proceed, the Chartermaster is replaced by the first Alderman Chamberlain.

**Diplomatic emissaries** scale with land-holder tier. The **League Emissary** (appointed at LH2 or by the League Secretary) handles peace and topic agreements. The **League Envoy** (appointed by the League Secretary) makes first contact with foreign realms. At higher tiers, a **High Emissary** (LH3) and **Grand Emissary** (LH4) take over these functions with correspondingly greater authority.

**The Merchant Master** (from population 100) leads the settlement's Merchant Guild, escorts caravans, and brokers trade agreements with the Alderman Quartermaster.

In **conquered settlements**, a **League Warden** is installed by the Archduke or the League Secretary to govern on the League's behalf. An **Inquisitor**, commanding fifteen constables, enforces order and roots out dissent within the occupied site.

## Lore ##

Two aldermen looked upon the scattered cities along the great coasts and decided that common law and shared purpose were worth more than independence alone. Together they codified the earliest laws of the League, established the right of free election, and set the foundations upon which everything that followed was built. In time, the weight of correspondence and diplomacy grew too great and so a league secretary was appointed - a steward of unity, ensuring the voices of the League's cities remained in accord.

As the League grew, so did the need for order within each city. Citizens elected aldermen to speak for them: a quartermaster to manage trade, a warden to keep the peace, a treasurer to guard the coin, a physician to tend the sick. Each alderman a voice; each election a small act of faith in the idea that the right person, chosen freely, could carry the burden of governance.

The first of those who truly earned their city's trust were given a new title: high alderman. As their cities flourished into ducal cities, these high aldermen became dukes - lawmakers who ruled not alone, but in council. Over time, councils of co-dukes emerged within the great cities, each duke risen from the ranks of aldermen by cunning political maneuvering.

It is not a clean system. Ducal politics are rich with tension, ambition, and the occasional quiet betrayal. A sufficiently gifted - or sufficiently cunning - individual may accumulate influence in multiple areas across trade, law, and coin with equal ease. Alliances shift. Loyalties are tested. The line between statecraft and scheming has always been thin.

And then, when one city grows so prosperous that no other can match it, when one grand duke's authority becomes undeniable - they are elevated. The first archduke. Supreme authority of the League, guiding its cities and its destiny.

The League endures not because it is perfect, but because enough of its citizens believe it is worth preserving.
